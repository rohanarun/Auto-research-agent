# An 8-by-4 full-rank final witness

## Scope

This is a conditional reduction of the final algebraic witness only. It assumes the same upstream generic-grid, negligible-height, and private-valuation conclusions used by the baseline 9-by-4 argument. It does **not** improve the headline unit-distance exponent.

The new observation is that, after the baseline additive gauge normalization, the differential system is homogeneous in all remaining differential data. Consequently the eight normalized right-hand coefficients are needed only projectively, costing at most seven field generators. Eight left points then suffice.

## 1. Imported hypotheses and notation

Work in the characteristic-zero algebraically closed ambient field of the upstream argument. After the anchor positions have been adjoined to the base field, let the left locus have dimension
\(d\in\{1,2\}\). For left points \(p_i\), right anchors \(r_\ell\), velocities \(H_i,U_\ell\), logarithmic velocities \(A_i,A'_\ell\), and nonzero radicals \(R_{i\ell}\), the imported differential equations are

\[
 2(p_i-r_\ell)\mathbin\cdot(H_i-U_\ell)
 =R_{i\ell}(A_i-A'_\ell),
 \qquad A_i-A'_\ell\ne0.                 \tag{1}
\]

We use the following precise consequences of the upstream hypotheses.

1. For an \(N\)-point generic left grid, adjoining a coefficient field of transcendence degree at most \(t\) beyond the base and anchor positions leaves joint left transcendence degree at least \(Nd-t\).
2. Once a left point \(p\) remains generic over the chosen coefficient field \(K\), the private-valuation construction applies to the four radicands \(s_\ell=R_\ell^2\in K(p)^\times\): there are valuations \(\nu_1,\ldots,\nu_4\) such that
   \[
   \nu_j(s_j)\equiv1\pmod2,
   \qquad \nu_j(s_\ell)\equiv0\pmod2\quad(\ell\ne j).       \tag{2}
   \]
3. In the full-rank branch, the four displacement vectors \(x_\ell=p-r_\ell\) span a two-dimensional space. This is the branch requiring three unknowns \((H_x,H_y,A)\), rather than the smaller common-line branch.

These are assumptions, not re-proved here.

## 2. Additive gauge normalization

Let \(J(x,y)=(-y,x)\), put \(v=r_2-r_1\), and note that \(v\cdot v\ne0\). Define

\[
 \omega=\frac{(U_2-U_1)\cdot Jv}{v\cdot v},
\]
\[
 \widetilde H_i=H_i-U_1-\omega J(p_i-r_1),\qquad
 \widetilde U_\ell=U_\ell-U_1-\omega J(r_\ell-r_1),             \tag{3}
\]
\[
 \widetilde A_i=A_i-A'_1,\qquad
 \widetilde A'_\ell=A'_\ell-A'_1.                              \tag{4}
\]

For \(x=p_i-r_\ell\), the extra rotational terms differ by
\(-\omega Jx\), and \(x\cdot Jx=0\). Thus (1) is unchanged, including its nonvanishing condition. Moreover

\[
 \widetilde U_1=0,
 \qquad \widetilde A'_1=0,
 \qquad \widetilde U_2=\tau v                                  \tag{5}
\]

for a scalar \(\tau\). After the anchor positions are in the base, all right-hand data are therefore represented by the eight entries

\[
 D=(\tau,\widetilde U_{3x},\widetilde U_{3y},
          \widetilde U_{4x},\widetilde U_{4y},
          \widetilde A'_2,\widetilde A'_3,\widetilde A'_4).     \tag{6}
\]

This recovers the baseline count \(5+3=8\).

## 3. The projective saving

Every term in the normalized version of (1) is homogeneous of degree one in the differential data. If \(D\ne0\), choose any nonzero coordinate \(q\) of \(D\), and divide **all** normalized left and right differential quantities by \(q\):

\[
 \widehat H_i=\widetilde H_i/q,
 \quad \widehat U_\ell=\widetilde U_\ell/q,
 \quad \widehat A_i=\widetilde A_i/q,
 \quad \widehat A'_\ell=\widetilde A'_\ell/q.                  \tag{7}
\]

Division of the normalized equation by \(q\) gives exactly

\[
 2(p_i-r_\ell)\cdot(\widehat H_i-\widehat U_\ell)
 =R_{i\ell}(\widehat A_i-\widehat A'_\ell),
 \qquad \widehat A_i-\widehat A'_\ell\ne0.                    \tag{8}
\]

The right coefficients are now \(D/q\). Its pivot coordinate equals one, so at most seven entries need be adjoined. Importantly, neither \(q\) nor the unscaled differential data need be adjoined: the subsequent argument uses only (8). As with the additive normalization, the hatted quantities need not themselves come from a new derivation.

If \(D=0\), no division is attempted and no right coefficient generator is needed. Thus in every case the coefficient cost is at most seven. This case split is essential: division by a fixed coordinate without first checking it is nonzero would be invalid.

Let \(K\) be the relative algebraic closure of the field generated by the upstream base, the four anchor positions, and either the seven projective ratios in \(D/q\), or no right data in the zero case. Then

\[
 \operatorname{trdeg}(K/\text{base plus anchors})\le7.          \tag{9}
\]

## 4. Eight points leave a generic point

Take \(N=8\). By the imported generic-grid inequality, the eight left points retain joint transcendence degree at least

\[
 8d-7.                                                          \tag{10}
\]

If no individual point were generic on its \(d\)-dimensional locus over \(K\), each would have transcendence degree at most \(d-1\). Subadditivity would then give joint transcendence degree at most

\[
 8(d-1)=8d-8,                                                   \tag{11}
\]

contradicting (10). Hence some left point \(p\) is generic over \(K\). This is the strict inequality that was absent before projectivization: with eight affine-normalized coefficients, the two sides would both be \(8d-8\).

Fix this \(p\), suppress its left index, and write \(R_\ell=R_{i\ell}\).

## 5. Private valuations and radical independence

Put \(F=K(p)\). From (2), the square classes of \(s_1,\ldots,s_4\) are independent in \(F^\times/F^{\times2}\). Indeed, if

\[
 \prod_{\ell=1}^4s_\ell^{e_\ell}\in F^{\times2},
 \qquad e_\ell\in\{0,1\},                                    \tag{12}
\]

then applying \(\nu_j\) modulo two gives \(e_j=0\) for every \(j\). Consequently

\[
 [F(R_1,R_2,R_3,R_4):F]=16,                                   \tag{13}
\]

and the sixteen radical monomials form an \(F\)-basis. In particular \(R_1,R_2,R_3\) are linearly independent over \(F\), and for each \(j\) there is an \(F\)-automorphism changing the sign of \(R_j\) alone.

This supplies both pieces required below: nonvanishing of the Cramer determinant and an automorphism that flips only the unused fourth radical.

## 6. The full-rank Cramer system

Use hatted data when \(D\ne0\), and the unchanged normalized data when \(D=0\); denote either choice by \(H,U_\ell,A,A'_\ell\). Put \(x_\ell=p-r_\ell=(x_{\ell x},x_{\ell y})\). Equation (8) becomes

\[
 (2x_{\ell x},2x_{\ell y},-R_\ell)
 \begin{pmatrix}H_x\\H_y\\A\end{pmatrix}
 =2x_\ell\cdot U_\ell-R_\ell A'_\ell.                         \tag{14}
\]

Because the four \(x_\ell\) span two dimensions, choose two nonparallel ones, put them among indices 1 and 2, choose either remaining anchor as index 3, and reserve the other as index 4. This reordering does not change the projective count. Let \([a,b]=a_xb_y-a_yb_x\). The determinant of the first three rows is

\[
 \Delta=-4\bigl([x_2,x_3]R_1-[x_1,x_3]R_2+[x_1,x_2]R_3\bigr),  \tag{15}
\]

up to the immaterial displayed global sign convention. Since \([x_1,x_2]\ne0\) and \(R_1,R_2,R_3\) are linearly independent over \(F\), \(\Delta\ne0\).

Cramer's rule applied to the first three equations therefore places

\[
 H_x,H_y,A\in L:=F(R_1,R_2,R_3),                               \tag{16}
\]

because every \(U_\ell,A'_\ell\) is in \(K\subset F\). Let \(\sigma\) be the automorphism of the full multiquadratic extension that fixes \(L\) and sends \(R_4\) to \(-R_4\). Applying \(\sigma\) to the fourth equation leaves its left differential unknowns and right coefficients fixed but reverses the radical. Subtracting the original equation gives

\[
 2R_4(A-A'_4)=0.                                               \tag{17}
\]

Since the characteristic is zero and \(R_4\ne0\), this says \(A=A'_4\), contradicting the nonzero difference in (8).

In the exceptional projective branch \(D=0\), the same proof applies with coefficient cost zero. In fact the first three right sides are zero, so invertibility already forces \(H_x=H_y=A=0\), while \(A'_4=0\), immediately contradicting \(A-A'_4\ne0\).

## 7. Conclusion and limits

Conditional on exactly the imported hypotheses stated in Section 1, the full-rank final contradiction needs four anchors and eight left points, hence

\[
 8\times4=32
\]

selected edges. The saving from 36 to 32 is a projectivization of the normalized differential coefficients, not a smaller common-line branch. No quantitative generic-grid extraction or negligible-height estimate is changed, so no improved headline unit-distance exponent is claimed.
