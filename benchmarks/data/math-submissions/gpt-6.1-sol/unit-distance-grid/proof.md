# An eight-by-four final algebraic witness

## Claim and scope

Under the same generic-grid and private-valuation hypotheses used in the snapshot, eight left points and four right anchors suffice for the final contradiction, including the full rank-two case. The selected-edge count is **32**. The improvement is a projective normalization of the homogeneous differential data, reducing the coefficient-field cost from eight generators to seven. No improvement of the headline unit-distance exponent is claimed.

The upstream extraction, negligible-height passage, and private-valuation lemma remain conditional dependencies. They are not proved or experimentally validated here. The original source is not present in this offline snapshot; the dependency interface below is the one asserted in `proof.tex`, made explicit. Our algebraic argument starts at that interface.

## Imported interface

Work in characteristic zero in the algebraically closed ambient field of the snapshot. After adjoining the four anchor positions to the upstream base, let F0 be its relative algebraic closure. There is a common irreducible left locus X of dimension d in {1,2}, and eight selected left points p_i satisfy

    trdeg_F0 F0(p_1,...,p_8) = 8d.

The right anchors r_1,...,r_4 are distinct. For v=r_2-r_1, v·v is nonzero, as in the snapshot's real finite-index argument. The selected edges supply nonzero radicals R_il and data H_i,U_l,A_i,B_l satisfying

    2(p_i-r_l)·(H_i-U_l) = R_il(A_i-B_l),    A_i-B_l != 0.       (1)

Here B_l denotes the snapshot's A'_l. The other imported input is the private-valuation statement used in the snapshot: after the coefficient-field enlargement, for a left point p remaining generic over that field K, the four radicands D_l=R_l^2 in F=K(p) have private odd valuations. Precisely, for each l there is a discrete valuation nu_l on F with nu_l(D_l) odd and nu_l(D_m) even for m != l. This is the source lemma applied after coefficient enlargement, not a claim that square-class independence survives arbitrary field extensions. The same upstream positional hypotheses and the same application of that lemma as in the baseline are retained below.

In particular, we do not adjoin the entire negligible-height field, the original left velocities, or the original normalization factors. As in the snapshot, the transformed velocities need only satisfy (1); they need not come from a new derivation.

## 1. The four-dimensional additive gauge

Write J(x,y)=(-y,x) and set

    omega = ((U_2-U_1)·Jv)/(v·v),
    H_i^0 = H_i-U_1-omega J(p_i-r_1),
    U_l^0 = U_l-U_1-omega J(r_l-r_1),
    A_i^0 = A_i-B_1,       B_l^0 = B_l-B_1.

The difference of the new velocities is H_i-U_l-omega J(p_i-r_l). Since q·Jq=0, (1) and all its nonzero scalar differences are preserved. Moreover,

    U_1^0=0,    B_1^0=0,    U_2^0=tau v,
    tau=((U_2-U_1)·v)/(v·v).

Thus, over F0, all normalized right data are described by the following eight scalars:

    T = (tau, U_3x^0, U_3y^0, U_4x^0, U_4y^0, B_2^0, B_3^0, B_4^0).     (2)

All anchor coordinates already belong to F0. This accounts for every velocity coordinate and every right logarithmic scalar; there is no omitted second coordinate of U_2^0.

## 2. Homogeneous scaling saves one generator

First suppose T is nonzero. Choose any index k with T_k != 0 and set c=T_k. Divide **all** four kinds of normalized differential data by c:

    H_i^*=H_i^0/c, U_l^*=U_l^0/c,
    A_i^*=A_i^0/c, B_l^*=B_l^0/c.                          (3)

Both sides of (1) are divided by c; the radicals and positions are unchanged. In particular A_i^*-B_l^* remains nonzero. The eight right parameters become T_j/c, and the kth parameter equals 1. Consequently the field generated by the entire right data over F0 is generated by at most the other **seven ratios**. Do not adjoin c separately. The chart index k is a choice among eight fixed integers, not a field generator. On overlapping charts the ratios change by rational functions, so choosing the chart adds no continuous parameter. Nothing later requires recovering the original velocities. The left scaled data may initially lie anywhere in the ambient field; the later invertible linear system, rather than an assumption about their field of definition, places them in the required radical subfield.

If T=0, take c=1 instead. The entire right data are zero and cost zero generators. This case is not discarded, and division by zero is never used.

Let K be the relative algebraic closure of F0 adjoined with these ratios (or K=F0 in the zero case). Then

    trdeg_F0 K <= 7.                                      (4)

The ratios lie in the field generated by the eight original normalized parameters. This is a smaller coefficient field of precisely the same kind as the baseline's field; the projective normalization introduces no additional positional or valuation assumption. We invoke the same imported private-valuation lemma directly over K, once a generic point over K has been found. We do not infer independence merely by extending constants or by counting generators.

## 3. Eight left points guarantee a surviving generic point

The tower formula gives

    trdeg_K K(p_1,...,p_8) >= 8d-7.

Indeed the compositum with K still has transcendence degree at least 8d over F0, and K costs at most seven. If every p_i failed to be generic in X over K, each individual transcendence degree would be at most d-1. Subadditivity would give at most 8(d-1)=8d-8, a contradiction. Hence some p is generic over K. Algebraic closure of the coefficient field does not change these transcendence-degree counts.

For comparison, adjoining all eight unscaled parameters would give only 8d-8; that equality cannot prove survival. Scaling, rather than a changed interpretation of this boundary, is what saves the row.

Fix this p, and abbreviate its left data by H,A and its four radicals by R_l. From this point all right data U_l,B_l lie in K and all q_l=p-r_l lie in F=K(p). Each q_l is nonzero, since a positive-dimensional generic point over K cannot equal an anchor in K.

## 4. Radical independence and the sign automorphisms

Apply the imported private valuations over F to D_l=R_l^2. If a product product_l D_l^{e_l}, e_l in {0,1}, were a square in F and e_j=1, its nu_j valuation would be odd. Squares have even valuation. Thus all e_l must be zero: the four classes [D_l] are independent in F*/F*2.

For completeness, independence implies that adjoining the four radicals gives degree 16 and admits each individual sign change. An elementary induction proves this. Suppose the first m radicals give a degree 2^m extension with its sign group and monomial basis. If a square root z of D_{m+1} lay in that extension, every sign automorphism would send z to +/-z. Thus z would be a common eigenvector of that group. The monomial basis has distinct characters, so z would equal a nonzero F multiple of one radical monomial. Squaring would make [D_{m+1}] a product of the first m square classes, a contradiction. This proves the induction. The base case is immediate. Therefore

    M=F(R_1,R_2,R_3,R_4)

has basis consisting of the 16 squarefree radical monomials, and the automorphism flipping R_l while fixing all other radicals exists. In particular distinct single radicals are linearly independent over F. This is the algebraic fact needed below, not numerical separation of square roots.

## 5. Full rank two: three equations determine all three unknowns

Suppose the four row vectors q_l span dimension two over F. Relabel the anchors, if necessary, so q_1 and q_2 are independent, and choose any third anchor distinct from them; the fourth is reserved for the sign change. Relabeling here does not change K or its generator count: all right data are already in K.

Put [a,b]=a_x b_y-a_y b_x. The first three equations are

    M_3 (H_x,H_y,A)^t = b,
    row_l(M_3)=(2q_lx, 2q_ly, -R_l),
    b_l=2q_l·U_l-R_l B_l.                                (5)

Direct determinant expansion yields

    Delta = -4[q_2,q_3]R_1 + 4[q_1,q_3]R_2 - 4[q_1,q_2]R_3.   (6)

Its R_3 coefficient is nonzero, and the single radicals are independent over F. Hence Delta != 0. Cramer's rule therefore puts H_x,H_y,A in E=F(R_1,R_2,R_3), regardless of where those unknowns initially lived in the ambient field.

The fourth equation now belongs to M:

    2q_4·(H-U_4)=R_4(A-B_4).

The automorphism of M that changes only R_4 fixes both its left side and A-B_4. Subtracting the original equation from its image gives

    0 = -2R_4(A-B_4).

Characteristic zero and R_4 != 0 force A=B_4, contrary to the nonzero difference in (1). This proves the full rank-two contradiction with four anchors. It is not a common-line-only reduction.

The all-zero right-data branch is included: in that branch b=0, and the invertible system (5) already forces H_x=H_y=A=0, contradicting A-B_l != 0 because B_l=0.

## 6. Rank one is also covered by the same 32 selected edges

If the q_l span dimension one, use a nonzero vector w spanning them and write q_l=s_l w, with s_l in F nonzero. For example w=q_1 works. Let h=w·H and t_l=q_l·U_l, both with t_l in F. The equations become

    2s_l h-R_l A = 2t_l-R_l B_l.

The first two have determinant 2(s_2 R_1-s_1 R_2), which is nonzero by radical independence. Thus h,A belong to F(R_1,R_2). The third radical sign change forces A=B_3, again impossible. Rank zero cannot occur because all q_l are nonzero. This argument also covers the snapshot's common-line branch without requiring a stronger branch hypothesis. In the zero-data case the two-equation homogeneous system already forces A=0.

## Conclusion and verification limits

At most seven coefficient generators, eight independently generic left points, and four right anchors suffice. The full final witness therefore has 8*4=32 selected edges, conditionally on exactly the imported generic-grid and private-valuation input described above. The gain does not quantify the upstream extraction or improve the unit-distance exponent.

Run `python -B solution/checks.py` for exact symbolic checks and failure controls, together with the unchanged baseline suite. The eight chart Jacobians have rank seven, their radial scaling direction lies in the kernel, and retaining the pivot restores rank eight. An additional exact arithmetic fixture in Q(sqrt(2),sqrt(3),sqrt(5),sqrt(7)) executes Cramer inversion and the fourth sign change with rational basis coordinates. Its four ordinary prime valuations certify independence for that fixture only; it is not a numerical or upstream geometric example. The new checks establish the displayed gauge and scaling identities, generator accounting, rank-two and rank-one determinant and Cramer identities, formal multiquadratic sign actions, and explicit failures when essential premises are removed. They do not verify the upstream source lemmas, and a formal quotient-algebra model is not evidence that a particular upstream radicand tuple has independent square classes; that step is the conditional valuation proof above.
